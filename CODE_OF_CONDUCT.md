# OpenPool Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

OpenPool contributors, maintainers, and users commit to making participation in the project respectful, constructive, and welcoming. Everyone should be able to contribute without harassment or discrimination, regardless of experience, identity, background, disability, appearance, nationality, beliefs, or other personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

- Communicate with empathy, patience, and technical clarity.
- Critique ideas and changes, not people.
- Respect different experience levels and ask questions in good faith.
- Keep discussions focused on improving OpenPool and helping its users.
- Accept responsibility, apologize when appropriate, and learn from mistakes.
- Protect credentials, private object data, and other sensitive information shared during project work.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory, sexualized, insulting, or demeaning language or imagery.
- Personal attacks, deliberate misrepresentation, or publishing another person's private information.
- Sharing credentials, signed URLs, private object details, security reports, or other sensitive material without authorization.
- Any conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a professional open-source community.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, release communication, and other spaces where someone represents OpenPool. It also applies to private project communication when that communication concerns participation in the project.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report sensitive conduct concerns through the repository's [private reporting form](https://github.com/alwynou/openpool/security/advisories/new) and clearly label the submission as a code-of-conduct report. If GitHub does not make that form available to you, open a minimal public issue requesting a private contact channel without including the report details, credentials, or unrelated private data. Maintainers will review reports in context, protect reporter privacy where reasonably possible, and take proportionate action.

Possible actions include a private correction, a warning, removal of content, temporary participation restrictions, or a permanent ban. Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith is itself a violation of this policy.

Maintainers must recuse themselves from handling a report when they are directly involved. In that case, they should identify a neutral person to review the available evidence and recommend an outcome.
